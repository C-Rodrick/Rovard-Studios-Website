Health, at homein your life.

This is a self-initiated concept project, not client work. Vita House is a fictional company. Providers, locations, prices, testimonials and articles are invented, and nothing here is medical advice or a claim about real healthcare outcomes. Where the case study refers to audience insight, it is based on desk observation and design assumptions that would be validated with research before launch.

01
The Challenge

Healthcare's digital front door still feels like a waiting room.

The brief I set myself: reimagine the traditional healthcare digital experience for a modern U.S. primary care and wellness company, one that feels trustworthy and professional, but also warm, calm and human.

1.1

The brief at a glance

The company

Vita House combines preventative healthcare, primary care and wellness services through an accessible digital experience, with physical “houses” in six U.S. cities and virtual care in twelve states.

The audience

Young professionals, families and health-conscious adults who want a more modern, convenient and human healthcare experience.

The ask

A complete brand and digital experience: identity system, responsive website, appointment booking, patient dashboard and mobile versions, with accessibility as a first principle.

03
Brand Strategy

Care that feels like coming home.

The strategy is a single idea, expressed consistently in name, symbol, colour, language and interaction.

Positioning statement

For busy, health-conscious adults and families who want healthcare that fits their lives, Vita House is the primary care and wellness home that pairs unhurried clinicians with effortless digital access, so staying well feels as natural as coming home.

3.1

Name, idea and tagline

Name

Vita + House

Vita is Latin for “life”. House is where life happens, a place of belonging rather than an institution. Together they say: life, looked after at home.

Brand idea

Care that feels like coming home

Every touchpoint should lower shoulders: warm light, clear words, a door that is always open.

Tagline

Health, at home in your life.

Two meanings on purpose: health that lives comfortably in your day-to-day, and a healthcare home you feel at ease in.

3.3

Brand personality

Six traits. Each one has a design rule and a voice rule, so the personality shows up in decisions, not just adjectives.

3.4

Voice & tone

Vita House sounds like a thoughtful friend who happens to be very good at healthcare: calm, specific and kind. It never scares, scolds or hides behind jargon.

Principles

  • Say the useful thing first.
  • Use everyday words.
  • Name the next step.
  • Never blame the reader.

Reading level

Patient-facing copy aims for roughly an 8th-grade reading level, with short sentences and no unexplained acronyms.

We say“You're all set, Maya. We've sent the details to your email.”
We avoid“Appointment confirmed. Reference ID: 7741-A. Please arrive per policy.”
We say“Enter a phone number like (555) 555-0100 so we can send reminders.”
We avoid“Invalid input.”
04
Visual Identity

A doorway, a sprout and a little sunlight.

A sophisticated healthcare identity built from warm neutrals, soft green and deep charcoal, with clay and honey as quiet accents, and not a trace of clinical blue.

4.1

The logo: “The Open Door”

Construction. The arch is a half-circle on a rectangle, 52 units wide. The V is a single 7-unit stroke with round caps, centred on the arch. The sun sits one stroke-width above the V, surrounded by a ring of breathing room that becomes the logo's signature “halo”.

4.2

Wordmark and lockups

Soft serif

Fraunces, with its “soft” optical setting, gives the name warmth without losing professionalism.

Roman + italic

Vita stands upright; House leans in, like a person in a doorway. The pair carries the brand's human voice.

Title case, never all-caps

Friendly and legible at every size. The name is never shouted, hyphenated or abbreviated.

Logo on colour

4.4

Clear space, minimum size and misuse

Clear space equals twice the stroke width of the V (“x”) on every side.

Minimum sizes

When the logo is not enough

On photography or busy backgrounds, place the logo on a solid Linen or Moss plate. Never place it directly over faces.

  • Use the symbol alone below 96px wide
  • Use the mono version on single-colour print

              12.1

              Reflection and next steps

              What worked

              One simple idea (the open door) gave every decision a test. The warm palette, soft serif and arch graphic system made a healthcare brand that feels distinctive without being cold.

              What I'd do next

              Test the booking flow with real people from each persona, commission photography from the art-direction studies, and review all copy with clinical and legal advisors.

              What this demonstrates

              Identity work and complex, user-centred product design can come from the same system: strategy, brand, UX, UI and a working front-end, all connected.
